About

Leadership, culture, and the choice to lead.

I am a social and organizational psychologist who studies leadership, with a particular focus on attitudes toward leadership. I completed my Ph.D. in Social and Organizational Psychology at Koç University in 2025, where I was advised by Prof. Dr. Zeynep Aycan.

My research examines how people interpret leadership across different social settings and how these interpretations relate to their attitudes toward taking on leadership roles. In particular, I investigate how people evaluate whether they would belong in leadership roles and be accepted as leaders across different collectives. This perspective allows me to address a central question for leadership research and practice: why do some individuals, sometimes even highly capable ones, refrain from pursuing leadership roles?

My work examines why some people come to see leadership as a role they can pursue, while others do not. I focus on how self-comparisons with existing leaders, perceived fit with shared beliefs, values, and expectations, and cultural cues such as symbols, jargon, policies, and practices relate to people’s attitudes toward taking on leadership in different social settings. My dissertation examined these questions across two real-life domains: organizations and local politics. In organizational settings, I studied employees’ perceived fit with existing leadership styles and their worries about leadership; in political settings, I examined local political candidates’ perceived similarity to party leaders, campaign engagement, and leadership worries. My broader program develops a multilevel theory of leadership culture: the shared and often implicit beliefs, values, and expectations that shape who sees themselves as fitting and acceptable candidates for leading.

Methodologically, I use field studies, survey-based structural equation and multilevel modeling, and experimental designs. I have also contributed to an interdisciplinary virtual-reality project examining whether immersive leadership simulations can reduce worries about leadership.

Research

What I work on

How people evaluate whether they can pursue leadership roles, belong in them, and be accepted as leaders.

01
👥

Self-Comparisons & Leadership

How people compare themselves with existing leaders, and how these comparisons relate to perceived fit, psychological safety, and leadership attitudes.

02
🏛️

Leadership Cultures

How shared beliefs, values, and expectations about leadership develop within groups, organizations, and societies, and shape who comes to see leadership as desirable and accessible for themselves.

03
🌍

Culture & Leadership Emergence

How leadership is understood and experienced across societal and organizational contexts, including work on paternalistic leadership, collectivism and individualism, and cross-cultural perspectives on leadership emergence.

04
⚥

Gender & Leadership Cues

Whether gendered leadership cues embedded in organizational environments relate to perceptions of who is suitable, accepted, or legitimate in leadership roles, as well as to leadership-related worries.

05
🥽

VR & Leadership Simulations

As part of an interdisciplinary research project, I contributed to the development and implementation of virtual-reality-based leadership simulations examining whether immersive simulations can be used to reduce leadership-related worries.
